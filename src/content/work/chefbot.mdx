## The problem

A half-empty fridge and no plan is an ordinary evening problem, and it is a poor fit for a recipe site. Recipes start from a complete ingredient list; the person starts from three things on a shelf and whatever is in the cupboard. When nothing obvious can be made, the food is thrown away and dinner is ordered in.

ChefBot starts from the other end. The person shows it what they have, and it works out a meal from that. The design intent is practical: food that gets cooked is food that is not wasted, and someone who is walked through a method a few times starts to learn it.

## What I did

- Designed the retrieval, the prompt logic and the multi-step tool flows of a cooking assistant built on GPT, and published it on the GPT store under my own name.
- Made the central path one flow: a photo of the fridge, ingredient recognition, a recipe, dietary adaptation and a PDF guide, run from a single message.
- Wrote the conversation starters as entry points to that flow, including “Bored? Take a photo of your fridge and we'll make something!”.
- Iterated the experience using usage patterns and the written feedback that OpenAI forwards to a GPT’s builder.

I did not train the underlying language model. ChefBot is built on GPT, and my work is the orchestration around it.

## Decisions that mattered

<dl class="decisions">
  <div>
    <dt>Start from a photo</dt>
    <dd>Typing out the contents of a fridge is a chore, and people forget half of it. A photo is one action. Recognising the ingredients is the first stage of the flow, so the person never has to write a list, though a typed list works too.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>One flow, no hand-holding</dt>
    <dd>Automated here means something specific: recognition, recipe, dietary adaptation and the PDF guide follow from one message, in order, without the person steering each step. The person stays free to interrupt and change course.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Sparse input is the normal case</dt>
    <dd>A thin ingredient list is what the assistant is designed for. It is asked to make something good from what is there, and the conversation builds from whatever the person has.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Dietary needs change the recipe</dt>
    <dd>A recipe someone cannot eat is no use to them. Dietary adaptation is a stage of the flow, and one of the four conversation starters asks for dietary needs outright.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>End with something to keep</dt>
    <dd>The last stage writes the recipe up as a PDF guide, so the result outlives the chat and can sit on a worktop while the person cooks.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Build on GPT, leave the weights alone</dt>
    <dd>The effort went into the flow around the model. This is a designed product on a third-party model, and I describe it that way.</dd>
  </div>
</dl>

## Reading the signals

The signals shown on this page are coarse: a conversation count, a ratings histogram and short written feedback. They are still enough to work from, read analytically. Which starter leads into the full flow, where a first answer falls short, what people praise in their own words and what they ask for: each points at a part of the flow to change.

## What people wrote

OpenAI emails a GPT’s builder when someone leaves written feedback. These are six such emails, quoted exactly as written, slips included. They are the support for the claim that ChefBot is useful to people: several describe cooking from what was already in the kitchen, and one describes growing confidence. They are six voices, chosen by no method, so they illustrate the intent and do not measure it.

## Limitations and status

ChefBot is live on the GPT store. It runs on a third-party platform that provides both its hosting and its model, so its availability is outside my control, and opening it requires a ChatGPT account. The figures are the rounded ones OpenAI shows on the listing, taken from screenshots supplied in September 2026. They count conversations, never people.

Less food waste and learning to cook are what the assistant is designed for and what the quoted feedback describes. Neither has been measured. The single exchange shown above was opened from the new-cuisine starter, so this page does not yet show the fridge-photo flow running end to end.

## What I learned

<div class="offset">

<p class="pull">With an assistant built on someone else’s model, the product is the flow.</p>

What it asks for, what it does next without being told, and what it leaves the person holding at the end: those were my decisions, and they are what people responded to. I also learned how far a few honest sentences of feedback go. The criticism quoted above is worth more to the next version than another five-star rating.

</div>
