## The hypothesis

Can an artist who does not exist, and whose records are made by API calls, earn a real audience? Cash Nova is a fictional producer (“I’m Cash, producer based in London”) that I created to find out. Generating a single track is the easy part. An album is a property of the whole set: a consistent sound, voices, a running order that flows. The hypothesis was that a pipeline could produce those qualities, and that listeners would respond to the result on its own terms.

## What I did

- Built and ran the pipeline that generated the catalogue programmatically through the <ExternalLink href="https://elevenlabs.io/eleven-music-api">Eleven Music API</ExternalLink> from ElevenLabs. No track was made by hand in a web interface.
- Released the output on ElevenMusic under the Cash Nova identity, with its own bio and artwork. On 15 September 2026 the profile showed 52 tracks and six albums, all released in 2026, among them Gold Coast, Burning In The Light, The Last Lie, Remixes and Midnight in Cartagena.
- Treated audience response as the outcome: a dated follower count, plus platform discovery and playlist placement, for which I publish no counts.

I did not build or train the generation model. It is ElevenLabs’ model, reached through their API. My work is the pipeline around it and the catalogue it produced.

## Technical stack

The tracks also received platform discovery and playlist placement; I publish no counts for either. According to my creator analytics the strongest-performing track is “Sun Comes Up” (feat. Wyclef Bonansi), on Gold Coast.

So far the answer to the question is: yes, a small audience. 97 followers and some playlist placement show that listeners found the music. They do not show more than that.

## Limitations and status

The figures belong to one platform and are modest. The only audience figure I publish is the follower count, with its date. The model is ElevenLabs’ and I make no claim about it. The pipeline code is not public. The stack above names the endpoints the pipeline is built on and how it approaches coherence across an album. It gives no measurements of how closely the tracks match, because I have none to publish.

## What I learned

Single generations are easy to come by. Consistency of sound, voice and order across a release has to be engineered, and that is where the pipeline earns its place. The audience figures are small, and they are the only measure I have of how the catalogue was received.
