## The problem

Diffusion-weighted MRI of the prostate is sensitive to geometric distortion, and an acquisition that looks plausible can still be unreliable for reading. My MSc thesis at UCL, October 2025 to August 2026, asked two questions. Can a model detect distorted acquisitions? And can it tell when its own judgement should not be trusted, so that the uncertain cases go to a person instead? The second question turned out to be the interesting one.

## What I did

- Built the experimental pipeline in PyTorch on 1,027 acquisitions from 627 patients (single centre, University College London Hospital, anonymised), under strict patient-level five-fold cross-validation.
- Benchmarked four backbones against classical radiomic baselines, over 700+ training runs, then trained a three-seed DenseNet-121 ensemble and measured how far its members agree.
- Built a heterogeneous nine-model ensemble, a retrospective triage simulation that defers the least-certain cases, and a distilled single model at about one-ninth of the ensemble’s inference cost.
- Reported with bootstrap confidence intervals and paired Wilcoxon tests; checked behaviour with Grad-CAM, calibration analysis and synthetic-distortion validation.

## The finding, and a plausible explanation

The three-seed DenseNet-121 ensemble was the most accurate model: mean AUROC 0.819, up to 0.17 above the classical radiomic baselines. Its uncertainty was close to useless. Uncertainty-error AUROC asks whether the cases a model is unsure about are the cases it gets wrong; 0.50 is chance. DenseNet-121 scored 0.52. ResNet-18, shallower and slightly less accurate, scored 0.64 (Figure 2).

A deep ensemble turns disagreement between its members into uncertainty, so I measured how much the members agree. DenseNet-121 models trained from different seeds make strongly correlated predictions: a mean pairwise correlation of 0.74, against 0.68 for ResNet-18 and 0.64 for ResNet-50. That ordering matches the ordering of the uncertainty results. It is consistent with the explanation that members which agree with one another, including when they are wrong, leave the ensemble little disagreement to report. It is an association across three architectures, not a demonstration of cause, and the thesis presents it as evidence for a plausible explanation and no more.

## Decisions that mattered

Three choices shaped every number here. The folds are drawn at patient level, because several acquisitions share a patient and splitting by acquisition would have leaked patient-specific appearance across folds. The ensemble was made heterogeneous, three architectures with three seeds each, because same-architecture members agreed too much; that lifted uncertainty-error AUROC from 0.522 to 0.685 and made deferral worth simulating (Figure 4). And the nine models were distilled into one student, because nine forward passes on every scan is not a deployable cost.

## Deferral, and a check against physics

An uncertainty signal earns its keep when it decides which cases a model should not answer. Selective prediction ranks the cases by uncertainty, defers the least certain, and measures accuracy on the rest: a tunable deferral rule, shown retrospectively, never run on live cases.

A classifier trained at one centre can learn a shortcut that has nothing to do with the artefact. To test that, I injected synthetic geometric distortion of increasing magnitude into undistorted test acquisitions.

## Limitations and status

This is a retrospectively validated proof of concept: a single centre, a triage simulation on held-out retrospective data, and nothing deployed or validated in a clinic. The fold-to-fold spread is large, as it will be with 627 patients, and the seed-correlation result is an association, not a cause. The results are evidence that the approach is worth carrying forward; they are not a clinical claim.

## What I learned

<div class="offset">

<p class="pull">Accuracy and self-knowledge are different properties, and optimising the first can quietly damage the second.</p>

An ensemble’s disagreement is only useful as an uncertainty signal if its members disagree in informative ways. I also learned to write a result down in its plainest form: spread across folds, splits at patient level, a simulation called a simulation, an association called an association.
