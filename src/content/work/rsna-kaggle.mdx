## The hypothesis

The 2026 RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection competition is a research code competition on Kaggle: multimodal knee-MRI classification of a defined set of clinically important abnormalities. It is still running, so this page reports a method, its status and my own held-out validation. There is no medal or leaderboard position to report.

My entry rests on a hypothesis about labels. Only 58 of the 4,407 training studies carry the 12 gold labels, which is far too few to train an image model on directly. But every training study comes with a free-text radiology report. If the reports can be turned into labels, the image model can learn from thousands of studies instead of dozens, and still run on the images alone when it is scored. The 58 gold studies are then worth more as a test than as training data.

## What I did

- Preprocessed about 500 GB of raw DICOM in shards on Kaggle, writing windowed, rescaled uint8 arrays at 384 pixels, and synced the result to a GPU cluster.
- Laid the pipeline out as four stages: report, labels, image model, image-only inference.
- Built a first baseline over 2.5D slice stacks: a timm slice encoder feeding a BiGRU, then attention pooling, series aggregation and 12 logits, trained with DistributedDataParallel on two Quadro GV100 GPUs with binary cross-entropy, mixed precision, a cosine schedule and AUC logged per label.
- Moved from that baseline to the current model, a 2.5D ensemble of EfficientNetV2 and DINOv2, trained on 3× A100 80GB over more than 300 GPU-hours.
- Split the data with GroupKFold at patient level, so validation never sees a patient the model trained on.
- Held out the 58 image-verified gold studies and read the ensemble against them, label by label. The readout below is that comparison.

## Decisions that mattered

<dl class="decisions">
  <div>
    <dt>The report is the teacher</dt>
    <dd>With 58 gold-labelled studies the supervised route is too thin to trust. Every study has a report, so the report supplies the training signal and the image model is the student. At inference the report is gone: the model that is scored sees the MRI and nothing else.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Preprocess once, in shards</dt>
    <dd>About 500 GB of raw DICOM is windowed and rescaled to uint8 arrays at 384 pixels, shard by shard on Kaggle, and the result is synced to the GPU cluster. The training loop reads the prepared arrays.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Start with a baseline, then replace it</dt>
    <dd>The first model was a timm slice encoder with a BiGRU reading the slices in order. It proved the data path and the per-label logging. The approach then evolved into an ensemble of two different image encoders, EfficientNetV2 and DINOv2, still over 2.5D slice stacks.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Split by patient, before anything else</dt>
    <dd>GroupKFold at patient level, the same discipline as in my <a href="/work/miqa">MSc work on prostate MRI</a>. A split that lets one patient sit on both sides makes every later number unreliable, so it was settled first.</dd>
  </div>
  <div>
    <dt>Look at every label, study by study</dt>
    <dd>There are 12 labels and very few gold examples of each. A single averaged score would hide a label the model has not learned, so each label is tracked on its own and the readout shows whole studies, misses included.</dd>
  </div>
</dl>

## Limitations and status

Fifty-eight studies is a small test set, and the three cases in the readout are illustrations, not a sample. Label-cell accuracy at a fixed threshold is a blunt measure. Most labels are absent in most knees, so a model earns a good share of its cells by saying no, and 83.5% should be read with that in mind. The ensemble slips most on synovitis and effusion. None of this is a competition score: the competition is still running, and there is no leaderboard position or medal to report.

## What I learned

<div class="offset">

<p class="pull">Weak supervision moves the difficulty from the images to the reports.</p>

The question stops being how to train on 58 studies and becomes how far a label read from a report can be believed. The data engineering had to come first: until about 500 GB of DICOM is in a form the GPUs can consume, there is no model to discuss. The second lesson carried over from the MSc: the split comes first, because nothing measured afterwards can be trusted without it. The third is that a model is easier to judge one study at a time than through one averaged number.

</div>
