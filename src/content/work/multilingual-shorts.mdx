## The hypothesis

Does a short-form educational format transfer between languages, or does each audience need its own? I built a pipeline to find out: one concept goes in, and two localised videos come out, an English one for CleverFacts and a Polish one for Ogarnik. Over ten days in March 2026 it published {a.en.experiment_videos} videos on each channel. Six months later I exported the full analytics history of both and analysed it. This page reports what the data supports, and says where it stops.

## What I built

- An automated production pipeline: the videos were generated through APIs, not edited by hand, so one concept becomes a vertical video with a voice.
- A localisation stage: each concept is adapted for the Polish audience, then dubbed expressively, so the delivery survives the change of language.
- A measurement stage: an export of each channel’s full history through the YouTube APIs, an analysis script that reduces it to aggregates, and the charts on this page, drawn from that file at build time.

I did not build or train the generation models. My work is the pipeline around them, the two channels it produced and the analysis that measures them.

## Limitations and status

Nothing new has gone out since March 2026. The analysis is observational: the two markets differ in size, the platform’s distribution decides most of what any single video receives, and a concept was published once per language, so no pair is a controlled comparison. A correlation of {a.pairs.spearman} across {a.pairs.n} hand-matched pairs, with an interval of roughly {a.pairs.spearman_ci95[0]} to {a.pairs.spearman_ci95[1]}, is evidence of a loose relationship and nothing finer.

Views count plays, not people. Per-video retention curves are not in this export, the demographic reports are channel-level, and the API has no hour-of-day dimension, so nothing here speaks to publishing time. The production code is not public, and this page describes that side of the pipeline by layer and interface only.

## What I learned

<div class="offset">

<p class="pull">A format can transfer between languages while the hits do not.</p>

The same pipeline produced a working channel in both markets, with the same launch curve and the same dependence on the feed. Which concepts took off was a different matter in each language. If I ran it again I would publish fewer concepts more than once, so that a concept could be told from a lucky day, and judge each video on its first week, because the data says there is no second one.

</div>
